All the settings in this guide are in one place: open **Settings** (press the **Windows key and I**), go to **Bluetooth & devices** > **Touchpad**. If you don't see some of them, your laptop may not have what Windows calls a *precision touchpad*; in that case, look for the laptop maker's touchpad app or **More touchpad settings** on the same page.

## Slow down the cursor

The **Cursor speed** slider sets how far the pointer travels when your finger moves. Moving it to the left means the same shake moves the pointer less. It's separate from your mouse speed, so you can have a slow touchpad and a normal mouse.

Move it a couple of steps left and try it for a day. Too slow and long movements become tiring; you're looking for the point where small targets feel manageable.

## Touchpad sensitivity: what it actually does

Under **Taps** there's a drop-down called **Touchpad sensitivity**, with options from **Most sensitive** to **Low sensitivity**. The name suggests it sets how hard you have to press, but Microsoft's own documentation explains that it controls something more specific: how much the touchpad **ignores input just after you've typed** on the keyboard. A lower setting means the touchpad waits longer and ignores more after each key press.

That's useful with a tremor, because a hand hovering over the keyboard often brushes the touchpad by accident:

- If the pointer jumps or clicks while you're **typing**, choose **Low sensitivity**.
- If the touchpad feels **unresponsive straight after typing**, choose a more sensitive level.

It won't change how the touchpad behaves when you're only using the touchpad, so the other settings below matter more for that.

## Turn off tap-to-click

By default, a light tap on the touchpad counts as a click. With a tremor, that can mean clicks you never intended. Under **Taps** you can untick:

- **Tap with a single finger to single-click.** Without it, you click by pressing the touchpad down until it clicks (or pressing the buttons below it, if your laptop has them). A deliberate press is much harder to do by accident than a tap.
- **Tap twice and drag to multi-select.** This lets a quick double tap followed by a slide start a drag. Accidental double taps can move files or select text you didn't mean to, so many people with tremor are better off without it.
- **Tap with two fingers to right-click.** Turn it off if menus keep popping up unexpectedly.

To drag without tapping, press the touchpad down, keep it pressed and slide. If keeping it pressed is hard, [ClickLock](/clicklock-windows-11) works with the touchpad too.

## Switch off gestures you don't use

Two-, three- and four-finger gestures can be set off by a wobble:

- Under **Scroll & zoom**, untick **Pinch to zoom** if pages keep zooming by themselves. You can still zoom in a browser with **Ctrl and +**.
- Under **Three-finger gestures** and **Four-finger gestures**, set **Swipes** and **Taps** to **Nothing** if windows keep switching or minimising when you didn't ask.

Keep **Drag two fingers to scroll** on if you find it easier than the scroll bar; turn it off if pages scroll unexpectedly.

## Use a mouse or trackball instead, when you can

Many people with tremor find a separate mouse or trackball easier than any touchpad, because the hand and forearm can rest on the desk. If you plug one in, look for **Leave touchpad on when a mouse is connected** at the top of the touchpad settings. Turning it off stops a resting palm from moving the pointer while you use the mouse.

Our [main guide to using a mouse with a hand tremor](/mouse-hand-tremor) covers mice, trackballs, and how to rest your arm for a steadier hand.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best touchpad sensitivity for a tremor?**
If the pointer jumps while you type, **Low sensitivity**. Otherwise it makes little difference; cursor speed and tap settings matter more.

**I turned off tap-to-click and now I can't click.**
Press the touchpad down firmly until you feel or hear a click, or use the buttons below it if your laptop has them.

**My touchpad settings page has fewer options.**
Your laptop probably doesn't have a precision touchpad. Look for **More touchpad settings** or the laptop maker's touchpad app.

**Do these settings change my mouse?**
No. Cursor speed and tap settings here apply to the touchpad only. Mouse settings are under **Bluetooth & devices** > **Mouse**.

## A note on your health

These settings make a laptop easier to use; they don't treat a tremor. If your tremor is new, getting worse or affecting daily life, talk to your doctor.
