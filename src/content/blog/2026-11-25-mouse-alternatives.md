The main alternatives to a mouse are **joysticks**, **roller bar mice**, **touchpads**, **pen mice and tablets**, **touchscreens**, **head and eye tracking**, and **foot mice**, as well as moving the pointer from the keyboard. For a hand tremor, a large trackball or a joystick is often worth trying first, because the device stays still and your hand can rest. What suits you depends on which movements you control best.

## Start with what you control best

Every pointing device asks for a different movement. Before comparing products, notice:

- **Which is steadier:** your fingers, your whole hand, or your arm?
- **Does resting your hand** on something calm the shake?
- **What goes wrong now:** steering the pointer, or keeping still while you click?

Then look for a device that uses your steadiest movement and separates moving from clicking.

## Joysticks

A joystick mouse works like the control on an electric wheelchair, as AbilityNet, a UK technology charity, puts it: you push the stick, and the direction and how far you push set where the pointer goes and how fast. Buttons on the base do the clicks, and AbilityNet notes that extra buttons can be set up for things like double-click or cut and paste.

Why it can suit a tremor:

- **The base stays put,** and your hand can rest on it.
- **You can let go.** When you release the stick, the pointer stops and stays where it is while you press a button.
- **The grip is whole-hand,** which many people find steadier than fingertip control.

The University of Washington's DO-IT programme adds that a joystick may be an excellent choice for someone who already drives a wheelchair with one, because the skill carries over.

The trade-off is speed: steering a pointer across a large screen with a stick is slower than a mouse.

## Roller bar mice

A bar mouse sits in front of the keyboard. AbilityNet describes a bar that slides left and right and rolls up and down, worked with the thumbs or fingers, so your hands stay at the keyboard.

It was designed to reduce reaching and wrist strain. For a tremor, the appeal is that both forearms stay resting on the desk and either hand, or both, can steady the bar. Whether the light, rolling movement suits a shaky hand is very individual, so try before you buy.

## Touchpads

A separate touchpad is like the one on a laptop: you slide a finger to move the pointer, and tap or press to click. DO-IT notes that external touchpads are ideal for some people.

For tremor, opinions differ. Parkinson's UK says laptop touchpads can be harder than a mouse for people with tremor or limited fine finger control. If you do use one, the settings matter a great deal; see [touchpad settings for shaky hands](/touchpad-settings-tremor).

## Pen mice and graphics tablets

A pen mouse is held like a chunky marker and moved across the desk, with buttons on its shaft. A graphics tablet pairs a pen-like stylus with a flat pad; AbilityNet notes that many people find the stylus more comfortable to hold, though tablets are aimed more at design work.

If you can still write reasonably well, a pen may feel natural. If writing is one of the things your tremor makes hardest, it probably isn't the answer.

## Touchscreens

Touching the screen directly removes the pointer altogether. Parkinson's UK reports that many people with Parkinson's find tablets easier than desktop or laptop computers. AbilityNet's advice on tremors is more cautious: with fairly good arm and finger control a touchscreen may be worth trying, but with a pronounced tremor it can cause more difficulty.

On a touchscreen, Parkinson's UK suggests a stylus with a soft, larger tip may suit people with tremor better than one with a small, hard tip.

## Head and eye tracking

If no hand-operated device works, the pointer can follow another part of you.

- **Head tracking** uses a camera, or a small device worn like glasses, to move the pointer as you move your head. DO-IT recommends considering it for people with good head control who can't use a pointing device with any limb. A switch does the click.
- **Eye tracking** follows where you look, and clicks when your gaze rests on a spot. AbilityNet notes it is typically much faster than head tracking and has become relatively affordable.

These take time to set up and learn. They're mainly for people whose hands can't manage any device, but it helps to know they exist.

## Foot mice

AbilityNet also describes foot mouse systems: one foot moves the pointer with a slip-on puck, and the other presses switches for clicks. DO-IT mentions an even simpler version, which is an ordinary mouse or trackball placed on the floor.

## The keyboard as a mouse

You may not need a new device for everything. [Mouse Keys](/mouse-keys-windows-11) moves the pointer with the number pad, one small step at a time, which is slow but very precise. And [keyboard shortcuts](/windows-keyboard-shortcuts) remove a lot of pointing altogether.

## How to choose

1. **Try the common options first.** A [trackball](/trackball-for-tremors) and a [vertical mouse](/vertical-mouse-tremor) are the easiest to find.
2. **Try before you buy** where you can. AbilityNet notes that a new device often feels clumsy at first, so give each one a fair trial.
3. **Keep your settings.** A slower pointer and an easier double-click help with every device on this page.
4. **Support your arm.** See [forearm and wrist supports for computer use](/forearm-support-for-computer).
5. **Get advice.** An occupational therapist may have devices you can try. In the UK, AbilityNet's free helpline is 0300 180 0028.

Our [overview of assistive technology for hand tremors](/assistive-technology-for-tremors) shows where pointing devices fit alongside keyboards, voice control and software.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best alternative to a mouse for shaky hands?**
There's no single best one. Trackballs and joysticks are the most common choices for tremor, because the device stays still and your hand can rest on it.

**Can I use a game controller or joystick as a mouse?**
Joysticks made for computer access are built to work as a mouse. A games joystick or controller generally needs extra software before it can move the pointer.

**Do these devices work with Windows 11?**
Devices that connect as a mouse, such as trackballs, joystick mice, bar mice and touchpads, work with the same Windows mouse settings. Eye tracking needs a compatible eye tracker.

**Can I have more than one device plugged in?**
Yes. Many people keep a mouse and a second device connected and use whichever suits the task.

## A note on your health

A different pointing device makes the computer easier to use; it doesn't treat a tremor. If your tremor is new, getting worse or affecting daily life, talk to your doctor. An occupational therapist can help you find equipment that suits you.
