The most helpful features in a keyboard for hand tremors are **larger keys**, which give you a bigger target, and a **keyguard**, a plate with holes over the keys that stops you pressing two at once and lets you rest your hands. **Compact** or **split** designs suit some people too. Before you buy anything, though, try the free Windows settings that work with any keyboard.

## Start with free settings

Whatever keyboard you use, Windows 11 can make it more forgiving:

- [Filter Keys](/filter-keys-windows-11) ignores doubled and accidental presses.
- [Sticky Keys](/sticky-keys-windows-11) lets you press shortcuts one key at a time.
- [Text suggestions](/text-suggestions-windows-11) and [voice typing](/voice-typing-windows-11) mean fewer keys to press.

For many people these settings, plus a steady arm position, make enough difference. If they don't, a different keyboard is the next step. Our tips for [typing with a hand tremor](/typing-with-hand-tremor) cover the settings and posture side in more detail.

## Larger keys

Keyboards with larger keys give your finger a bigger area to land on. AbilityNet, a UK charity that helps disabled people use technology, describes them as easier to see and to press. The University of Washington's DO-IT programme notes that extra-large keys can work well for someone with a good range of movement but poor fine control, which is how a tremor often affects the hands.

Things to check:

- **Key size and spacing.** Bigger keys with clear gaps between them are easier to hit than bigger keys packed tightly together.
- **Clear lettering.** High-contrast colours, such as black on yellow, make letters easier to find. AbilityNet mentions coloured keyboards and key stickers as options.
- **Size on the desk.** Large-key keyboards take more space, which can mean reaching further for the mouse.

## Keyguards

A keyguard is a rigid plastic or metal plate that fits over the keyboard, with a hole above each key. Parkinson's UK explains that it stops you pressing two keys at once, and that you can rest your hands and arms on it without pressing anything.

For many people with a tremor, the resting part matters as much as the accuracy: your hand can settle on the plate, then a finger drops through the hole to press the key. A keyguard has to match the exact keyboard model, so it's often easiest to buy a keyboard and keyguard together.

## Compact keyboards

A compact keyboard leaves out the number pad on the right. AbilityNet notes that this takes up less space and stops you stretching too far, for example to reach the mouse. If you need numbers often, a separate number pad can sit on either side, or be moved away when you don't need it.

Keeping the mouse closer to your body can also help, as our [guide to using a mouse with a hand tremor](/mouse-hand-tremor) explains.

## Split and ergonomic keyboards

Ergonomic keyboards are shaped to reduce hand and wrist movement. Some are curved; some come in two halves you can place separately. Parkinson's UK lists curved and two-part designs among the keyboards that can help. They take some getting used to, so try one before you buy if you can.

## A steady keyboard on a steady desk

Small things help too:

- **A keyboard that doesn't slide.** Rubber feet, or a non-slip mat underneath, stop it moving when you press keys.
- **A separate keyboard for a laptop.** Plugging in a full keyboard lets you set up the desk so your arms can rest.
- **Space in front of it.** Room on the desk to rest your forearms gives your hands a steadier base. Many people find this reduces how much the tremor gets in the way.

## How to choose

1. **Note your most common mistakes.** Wrong key next door? Look at larger keys or a keyguard. Doubled letters? Filter Keys may be enough.
2. **Try before you buy** if you can, or buy from a shop with an easy return policy. What works is very personal.
3. **Ask for help.** An occupational therapist can advise on equipment and set-up. In the UK, AbilityNet runs a free helpline and can arrange volunteer help at home.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best keyboard for hand tremors?**
There isn't one best keyboard for everyone. Many people with a tremor do well with larger keys, a keyguard, or both. Free settings like Filter Keys help with any keyboard.

**Do keyguards work with any keyboard?**
No. A keyguard is made to fit a particular keyboard model, so check that the two match.

**Is a wireless keyboard all right?**
Either kind works. Choose whichever sits most steadily on your desk and suits your set-up.

**Would a touchscreen be easier than a keyboard?**
For some people, yes; Parkinson's UK says many people with Parkinson's find tablets easier. AbilityNet notes that a strong tremor can make touchscreens harder. If you can, try one first.

## A note on your health

A different keyboard can make typing easier; it doesn't treat a tremor. If your tremor is new, getting worse or affecting daily life, talk to your doctor. They can also refer you to an occupational therapist.
