## What ClickLock does

Normally, to drag something you hold the main mouse button down the whole time you move. With ClickLock turned on:

1. You **press and hold** the button for a moment, then let go. Windows "locks" the button as if you were still holding it.
2. You **move the mouse** to drag or select. Your finger can rest; the button stays locked.
3. You **click once more** to let go.

It works anywhere in Windows: moving files, selecting text, dragging a window by its title bar, resizing a window from its edge, and dragging a scroll bar. ClickLock is switched off by default.

## How to turn on ClickLock in Windows 11

1. Open **Settings** (press the **Windows key and I**).
2. Go to **Bluetooth & devices** > **Mouse**.
3. Under **Related settings**, select **Additional mouse settings**. The **Mouse Properties** window opens.
4. On the **Buttons** tab, tick **Turn on ClickLock**.
5. Select **OK**.

If you prefer the old Control Panel, the same window is under **Control Panel** > **Mouse**.

## Choose the right hold time

Next to **Turn on ClickLock** is a **Settings** button (it's greyed out until ClickLock is ticked). It opens a slider from **Short** to **Long**, which sets how long you must hold the button before it locks.

This setting is the key to making ClickLock work with a tremor, and it's a trade-off:

- **Shorter:** the lock comes on quickly, so you don't have to hold steady for long. But if you tend to press slowly or rest on the button, you may lock it when you only meant to click.
- **Longer:** ordinary clicks never lock by accident. But you need to keep the button pressed a little longer before you can let go.

A sensible way to find your setting: start in the middle, use the PC normally for a day, then move the slider one step. If you keep locking by accident, go longer. If holding is the hard part, go shorter.

## Using ClickLock day to day

**Moving a file into a folder**

1. Point at the file. Press and hold until it locks, then let go.
2. Move the pointer over the folder. Take your time; nothing drops until you click.
3. Click once to drop it.

**Selecting text**

1. Point just before the first word. Press and hold until it locks, then let go.
2. Move to the end of the text you want. The selection grows as you move.
3. Click once to finish the selection.

**Moving or resizing a window**

Lock on the window's title bar to move it, or on its edge or corner to resize it, then click again when it's where you want it.

If you drop something in the wrong place, press **Ctrl and Z** straight away. In File Explorer and most apps, that undoes the move.

## When ClickLock gets in the way

- **Things lock when you only meant to click.** Make the hold time longer, as above.
- **You forget it's locked.** If the pointer seems to be dragging something, click once to release it.
- **It applies to your touchpad too.** ClickLock is a Windows-wide setting, so it also affects a laptop touchpad. If that's a problem, see the touchpad's own tap-and-drag setting instead.
- **It doesn't seem to work.** Some mice come with their own software, which can override Windows button settings. Check the mouse maker's app if ClickLock does nothing.

## Ways to avoid dragging altogether

ClickLock makes dragging easier, but many drags can be replaced with keys:

- **Moving files:** select the file, press **Ctrl and X** (cut), open the destination folder, press **Ctrl and V** (paste).
- **Selecting text:** click at the start, then hold **Shift** and press the arrow keys. **Shift and End** selects to the end of the line.
- **Moving a window:** press the **Windows key** with the left or right arrow to snap it to one side of the screen.

If pressing two keys at once is hard, [Sticky keys](/windows-11-accessibility-tremor) lets you press them one after the other.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is ClickLock the same as holding the mouse button down?**
To your apps, yes: they see the button held down. The difference is that your finger doesn't have to keep pressing.

**Does ClickLock help with double-clicks?**
No, it only changes holding and dragging. For stray double-clicks, see [How to stop accidental double-clicks in Windows](/accidental-double-click).

**How do I turn ClickLock off?**
Go back to the **Buttons** tab in **Mouse Properties**, untick **Turn on ClickLock** and select **OK**.

## A note on your health

ClickLock makes the computer easier to use; it doesn't treat a tremor. If your tremor is new, getting worse, or making everyday tasks harder, talk to your doctor. For more ideas, our [main guide to using a mouse with a hand tremor](/mouse-hand-tremor) covers settings, posture and equipment.
