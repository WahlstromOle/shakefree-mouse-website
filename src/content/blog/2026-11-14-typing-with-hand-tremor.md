The quickest wins for typing with a hand tremor are: turn on **Filter Keys** so Windows ignores shaky presses, rest your forearms so your hands have a steady base, and type less by using word suggestions or your voice. If the keyboard itself is the problem, a different keyboard or a keyguard can help too.

Here's each of those in a little more detail, starting with the easiest.

## 1. Let Windows ignore the shaky presses

Windows can't stop your hands shaking, but it can ignore many of the mistakes the shake causes.

- **Doubled letters or stray keys:** turn on [Filter Keys](/filter-keys-windows-11). Its **Ignore unintended keystrokes** option stops one press turning into two. AbilityNet, a UK charity that helps disabled people use technology, recommends Filter Keys to people with tremors for exactly this.
- **Keys that run on when you rest on them:** make Windows wait longer before a held key repeats. Press the **Windows key**, type **keyboard**, open **Keyboard**, and on the **Speed** tab move **Repeat delay** towards **Long**.
- **Shortcuts that need two keys at once:** turn on [Sticky Keys](/sticky-keys-windows-11), so you can press **Ctrl**, then **C**, one key at a time.

All three are free and built into Windows 11. Our [guide to Windows 11 accessibility settings for hand tremors](/windows-11-accessibility-tremor) shows where they are.

## 2. Give your arms a steady base

Many people find a tremor is smaller when the arm has something solid to rest on, rather than hovering over the keys.

- Rest your forearms on the desk, or on chair armrests at the right height.
- Keep the keyboard close, so you aren't reaching forward.
- Try resting the heels of your hands on a wrist rest, or on the edge of a keyguard (see below).
- Sit so your shoulders are relaxed and your elbows are bent at about a right angle.

An occupational therapist can look at how you sit and suggest the right chair height and distance to the screen and keyboard. Parkinson's UK notes that you can be referred to one through your GP or social services, or see one privately.

## 3. Type less

Every key you don't need to press is one less chance to slip.

- **Word suggestions.** Windows 11 can suggest the word you're typing after a few letters, so you pick the word instead of typing it out. Turn it on under **Settings** > **Time & language** > **Typing** > **Show text suggestions when typing on the physical keyboard**. Then press the **up arrow**, choose a word with the **left** and **right arrows**, and press **Enter**.
- **Your voice.** Press the **Windows key and H** in any text box and speak instead of typing. AbilityNet suggests voice recognition if your voice is clear; it takes a little patience to get used to, but it can take over a lot of typing.
- **Copy and paste what you type often.** Your address, email sign-off or a standard reply can live in a note, ready to copy.

## 4. Make corrections quicker

Mistakes will still happen. A few keys make fixing them less tiring:

- **Ctrl and Backspace** deletes the whole word to the left of the cursor, instead of one letter at a time.
- **Ctrl and Z** undoes the last change.
- **Ctrl and the left or right arrow** jumps a whole word at a time, so you can reach a mistake without the mouse.

With Sticky Keys on, you can press each of these one key at a time.

## 5. Check the keyboard itself

The keyboard you have may be making things harder than they need to be.

- **A separate keyboard on a laptop.** A full-size keyboard on the desk lets you rest your arms in a way a laptop often doesn't.
- **Larger keys.** Keyboards with bigger keys give you a bigger target. Parkinson's UK and AbilityNet both mention them for people who find it hard to hit the right key.
- **A keyguard.** This is a rigid plate with a hole over each key. Parkinson's UK explains that it stops you pressing two keys at once, and lets you rest your hands on the plate without typing anything.

If you can, try a keyboard before you buy it. What works is personal.

## 6. Work with your tremor, not against it

The NHS lists things that can make a tremor more noticeable, including tiredness, stress, anxiety, caffeine and being very hot or cold. You may notice your typing is better at some times of day than others.

- Save longer typing jobs for the times your hands are steadier, if you can.
- Take short breaks rather than pushing on when you're tired.
- Don't rush. Typing a little slower with fewer mistakes often ends up quicker.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best Windows setting for typing with a tremor?**
For most people, Filter Keys with **Ignore unintended keystrokes** turned on. It stops one shaky press from typing a letter twice.

**Is voice typing better than typing?**
It can be, especially for longer messages. Many people mix both: voice for writing, keyboard for small fixes.

**Will a special keyboard stop my mistakes?**
It can reduce them, but it won't stop them all. Settings like Filter Keys work with any keyboard, so start there.

**Are tablets easier?**
Some people find touchscreens easier, and Parkinson's UK says many people with Parkinson's do. Others find a strong tremor makes a touchscreen harder. Try one if you can before you buy.

## A note on your health

These tips make typing easier; they aren't medical advice and don't treat a tremor. The NHS suggests seeing a GP if your tremor is getting worse over time or affecting your daily activities, so talk to your doctor if that sounds like you. If you've been prescribed medicine, don't stop or change it without speaking to your doctor first.
