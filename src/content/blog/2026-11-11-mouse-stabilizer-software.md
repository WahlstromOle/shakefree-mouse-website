## How tremor smoothing works

A tremor is a rhythmic movement: the hand moves back and forth at a fairly steady pace. In essential tremor, the most common cause of a shaking hand during movement, research describes that pace as roughly **4 to 12 shakes per second**, and for any one person it tends to stay much the same over long periods, while the size of the shake can change from hour to hour.

Most deliberate mouse movements are slower and smoother than that. Smoothing software uses this difference. It watches the pointer's movement and filters out the fast back-and-forth part, while letting the slower, intended movement through. The result is a pointer that follows where you mean to go, with much less of the jitter.

There's always a trade-off, and it's the most important thing to understand:

- **More smoothing = a steadier pointer, but more lag.** To tell a shake from a real movement, the software has to look at a short stretch of movement, so the pointer trails slightly behind your hand.
- **Less smoothing = a quicker pointer, but more shake gets through.**

That's why good smoothing software lets you choose the strength, and why the best setting is different for everyone, and sometimes different on a good day and a bad day.

## What the research says

Filtering tremor out of mouse movement isn't a new idea. A few examples from published research:

- In a study of **36 people with multiple sclerosis** and tremor, a motion-filtering program helped participants complete computer tasks such as double-clicking faster, more of them could finish all the tasks, and users said it was less tiring and more comfortable than a standard mouse. People with marked tremor during movement seemed to benefit most (Feys and colleagues, 2001).
- IBM researchers built an **adapter that sat between the mouse and the computer** and smoothed movement, ignored accidental clicks and made double-clicking easier. In a preliminary test with people with essential tremor, most reported moderate to considerable improvement (Levine and Schappert, 2005).
- Other researchers have built devices that learn to tell voluntary movement from tremor in real time, and tested them with users (Rocon and colleagues, 2006).

These studies are small and some are old, and they tested their own systems rather than any product you can buy today. They show that the approach can help; they don't prove that any particular app will help you. Trying it yourself, ideally with a free trial, is the real test.

## What smoothing software can do

- **Steady the pointer** so it's easier to land on small buttons, links and menu items.
- **Make clicking more accurate,** especially combined with click features (see below).
- **Work with the mouse, trackball or touchpad you already have,** because it works on the movement, not the device.
- **Reduce effort.** If you don't have to fight the pointer, computer work can be less tiring.

## What it can't do

- **It doesn't treat your tremor.** It changes what the pointer does, not what your hand does. Treatment is a conversation for your doctor.
- **It can't read your mind.** Smoothing separates movement by how fast it changes. Sudden, irregular jerks, or a slow drift in the wrong direction, look like deliberate movement to a filter and may come through.
- **It adds some lag.** At high strength the pointer feels slower, a bit like moving through honey. Most people get used to it, but it's a real cost.
- **It doesn't fix clicks on its own.** A hand can shake while pressing the button, moving the pointer off target or pressing twice. That needs separate click features, like those in the next section, or settings such as [ClickLock](/clicklock-windows-11).
- **It doesn't help with typing.** For doubled letters, use Windows' **Filter keys** (see [Windows 11 accessibility settings for hand tremors](/windows-11-accessibility-tremor)).
- **It doesn't work on touchscreens and phones,** where you touch the screen directly.

## What to look for

If you're comparing options, these are worth checking:

- **Adjustable strength,** so you can find your own balance between steadiness and speed.
- **A quick on/off,** ideally a keyboard shortcut, for tasks where you want the pointer at full speed.
- **Click help:** ignoring accidental second clicks, and keeping the pointer still while you press.
- **Works system-wide,** in every app, not just one program.
- **Runs on your own PC** without sending your movements anywhere.
- **A free trial,** because only you can tell whether it feels right.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does mouse smoothing software work for Parkinson's tremor?**
Smoothing is designed to filter rhythmic shaking of the hand, rather than one particular condition. How much it helps varies from person to person, and the research above looked at people with essential tremor and multiple sclerosis, not Parkinson's. A free trial is the best way to find out for yourself.

**Will smoothing make my pointer slow?**
A little, especially at higher strength. Start low and increase it until the shake is manageable, and use a quick on/off for tasks that need speed.

**Is a stabilizer mouse better than software?**
They work differently. Heavier mice and trackballs help by giving the hand something steady to rest on; software filters the movement itself. Many people combine both.

**Is this a replacement for treatment?**
No. It's a tool for using the computer. Talk to your doctor about the tremor itself.

## A note on your health

Nothing here is medical advice. If your tremor is new, getting worse, or affecting your daily life, see your doctor. An occupational therapist can also help you find equipment and ways of working that suit you.
