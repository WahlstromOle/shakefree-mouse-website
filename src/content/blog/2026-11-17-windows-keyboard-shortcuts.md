The Windows keyboard shortcuts that save the most mouse work are **Ctrl and C / X / V** to copy, cut and paste, **Ctrl and Z** to undo, **Alt and Tab** to switch windows, **Alt and F4** to close one, and **Windows key and D** to show the desktop. Below they're grouped by task, with a few lesser-known ones that are especially useful when your hand shakes.

## First: make shortcuts easier to press

Most shortcuts need two keys held together. If that's hard, turn on [Sticky Keys](/sticky-keys-windows-11) so you can press them one at a time: **Ctrl**, then **C**. If keys get typed twice, [Filter Keys](/filter-keys-windows-11) helps too.

In this guide, "Ctrl and C" means press Ctrl and C together, or one after the other with Sticky Keys on.

## Copy, paste and undo

| Shortcut | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Ctrl and C** | Copy what's selected |
| **Ctrl and X** | Cut what's selected |
| **Ctrl and V** | Paste |
| **Ctrl and Z** | Undo the last action |
| **Ctrl and Y** | Redo what you just undid |
| **Ctrl and A** | Select everything |

**Ctrl and Z** is the one to remember. A stray click that moved a file or deleted a sentence can usually be put right straight away.

## Windows and apps

| Shortcut | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Alt and Tab** | Switch between open windows (keep Alt held and tap Tab to move along) |
| **Alt and F4** | Close the window you're in |
| **Windows key and D** | Show or hide the desktop |
| **Windows key and E** | Open File Explorer |
| **Windows key and I** | Open Settings |
| **Windows key and L** | Lock the PC |
| **Esc** | Close a menu or box without doing anything |

These replace some of the smallest targets on screen, such as the **X** in the corner of a window.

## Move and resize windows without dragging

Dragging a window by its title bar is hard with a tremor. These keys do it for you:

| Shortcut | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Windows key and up arrow** | Make the window fill the screen |
| **Windows key and down arrow** | Make it smaller, or minimise it |
| **Windows key and left arrow** | Snap it to the left half of the screen |
| **Windows key and right arrow** | Snap it to the right half |

## Editing text

Selecting text with the mouse means aiming at the exact start and end of a word. The keyboard is far more precise:

| Shortcut | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Ctrl and left / right arrow** | Jump a whole word left or right |
| **Shift and Ctrl and left / right arrow** | Select a word at a time |
| **Home** / **End** | Go to the start or end of the line |
| **Ctrl and Backspace** | Delete the whole word before the cursor |
| **Ctrl and F** | Find a word on the page or in the document |

A good routine: click roughly near the text, then use the arrow keys to get exactly where you want.

## Helpful extras

- **Windows key and V** opens your **clipboard history**: the last things you copied, so you can paste any of them. It's off at first; press **Windows key and V** and select **Turn on**, or go to **Settings** > **System** > **Clipboard**.
- **Windows key and H** starts [voice typing](/voice-typing-windows-11) in any text box.
- **Windows key and full stop (.)** opens the emoji panel.
- **Windows key and Shift and S** takes a screenshot of part of the screen.
- **Windows key and plus (+)** zooms in with Magnifier, and **Windows key and Esc** closes it.
- **Windows key and U** opens the Accessibility settings.

In your web browser, **Alt and left arrow** goes back a page. **Tab** moves between links and buttons, and **Enter** opens the one that's highlighted.

## How to remember them

- **Start with three.** Pick the three you'd use most, for example copy, paste and undo, and use only those for a week.
- **Keep a note by the screen.** A small printed card next to the monitor saves looking them up.
- **Add more slowly.** Once the first ones are habit, add the next two or three.

## For the mouse work that's left

Shortcuts cut down the clicks, but some things still need the mouse. Windows has settings that help, collected in our [guide to using a mouse with a hand tremor](/mouse-hand-tremor). For a steadier pointer, we built [ShakeFree Mouse](https://apps.microsoft.com/detail/9NXR1GRZ2P77), a small Windows app that smooths out shaking and filters accidental clicks; our guide to [mouse smoothing software](/mouse-stabilizer-software) explains what this kind of software can and can't do.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do these shortcuts work in every app?**
Most do. Copy, paste, undo and the Windows key shortcuts work almost everywhere. A few apps use their own shortcuts for some actions.

**I press Windows key and V and nothing is there.**
Clipboard history is off by default. Select **Turn on** when it asks, or turn it on under **Settings** > **System** > **Clipboard**. Items you copied before that won't appear.

**Can I make my own shortcuts?**
Some apps, such as Microsoft Word, let you set your own. Windows itself has a fixed set.

**Where is the full list?**
Microsoft keeps a complete list on its support site; it's linked in the sources below.

## A note on your health

Keyboard shortcuts make the computer easier to use; they don't treat a tremor. If your tremor is new, changing, or making everyday tasks harder, talk to your doctor.
