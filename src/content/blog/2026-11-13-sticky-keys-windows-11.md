To turn on Sticky Keys in Windows 11, press the **Windows key and U**, select **Keyboard** and switch on **Sticky keys**. You can also press **Shift** five times in a row. After that, press **Ctrl**, let go, then press **C**: Windows copies, just as if you'd held both keys.

Our [overview of Windows 11 accessibility settings for hand tremors](/windows-11-accessibility-tremor) introduces Sticky Keys briefly. This guide covers how it behaves, which options suit a tremor, and how to stop it appearing when you don't want it.

## How Sticky Keys works

Some keys are only ever used together with another key: **Shift**, **Ctrl**, **Alt** and the **Windows key**. These are called modifier keys. With Sticky Keys on:

- Press a modifier key once and let go. It stays "pressed" until you press the next, ordinary key.
- Press the next key. Windows treats it as the combination, then releases the modifier.

So for **Ctrl and Z** (undo) you press **Ctrl**, let go, then press **Z**. For a three-key shortcut such as **Ctrl, Alt and Delete**, press them one at a time in that order.

The difference with a tremor is that you never have to hold one key steady while aiming at another.

## How to turn on Sticky Keys

1. Press the **Windows key and U**. **Settings** opens at **Accessibility**.
2. Under **Interaction**, select **Keyboard**.
3. Turn on the **Sticky keys** switch.
4. Select the arrow next to the switch to see its options.

## The options, and which to choose

The Sticky keys page has five options. Here's what each does and what many people with a tremor choose.

**Keyboard shortcut for Sticky keys**
Lets you turn Sticky Keys on or off by pressing **Shift** five times. Useful if you only want it some of the time. If a tremor makes you tap Shift repeatedly by accident, turn this off so Sticky Keys doesn't keep switching itself.

**Show the Sticky keys icon on the taskbar**
Puts a small icon near the clock that shows which modifier keys are currently "pressed". Worth turning on while you get used to Sticky Keys, so you can see why a key is behaving differently.

**Lock shortcut keys when pressed twice in a row**
Press a modifier key twice and it stays locked on until you press it again. This is handy when you need the same key for several actions in a row, for example **Shift** for a run of capital letters, or **Ctrl** for several shortcuts.

**Turn off Sticky keys when two keys are pressed at once**
With this on, pressing two keys together switches Sticky Keys off, on the idea that you no longer need it. With a tremor, two keys often go down together by accident, which can switch Sticky Keys off without you noticing. Many people with a tremor find it better to leave this option off.

**Play a sound when a shortcut key is pressed or released**
A sound tells you whether a modifier key is now active. It's an easy way to know what state you're in without looking for the icon.

## Shortcuts worth trying with Sticky Keys

Once Sticky Keys is on, these replace a lot of mouse work:

- **Ctrl**, then **C** to copy, **Ctrl**, then **V** to paste.
- **Ctrl**, then **Z** to undo a slip.
- **Alt**, then **F4** to close the window you're in.
- **Windows key**, then **D** to show the desktop.

## How to turn off Sticky Keys, and stop the pop-up

If a box keeps appearing asking "Do you want to turn on Sticky Keys?", it's because **Shift** was pressed five times in a row. To stop it:

1. Press the **Windows key and U** and select **Keyboard**.
2. Select **Sticky keys** to open its options.
3. Turn off **Keyboard shortcut for Sticky keys**.

To turn Sticky Keys off completely, switch off the **Sticky keys** switch on the same page.

## Sticky Keys and Filter Keys together

If you also get doubled letters, Sticky Keys works well alongside [Filter Keys](/filter-keys-windows-11). Filter Keys ignores unwanted presses; Sticky Keys removes the need to hold keys together. Turn them on one at a time, so you know which change made the difference.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Sticky Keys work in every app?**
It's a Windows setting, so it works in Windows itself and in normal apps such as Word, your web browser and File Explorer.

**I pressed Ctrl by mistake and now letters do strange things. What happened?**
Ctrl is waiting for the next key, so the next key you press becomes a shortcut. The taskbar icon and the sound option help you spot this. If you locked Ctrl by pressing it twice, press it once more to release it.

**Can I use Sticky Keys on a laptop?**
Yes. It works with a laptop's built-in keyboard and any keyboard you plug in.

**My settings are greyed out.**
On a work PC, your organisation may manage these settings. Ask your IT team.

## A note on your health

Sticky Keys makes the keyboard easier to use; it doesn't treat a tremor. If your tremor is new, changing, or making everyday tasks harder, talk to your doctor. An occupational therapist can also suggest equipment and ways of working that suit you.
