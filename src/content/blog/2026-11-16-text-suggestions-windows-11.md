To turn on text suggestions in Windows 11, open **Settings** > **Time & language** > **Typing** and switch on **Show text suggestions when typing on the physical keyboard**. From then on, a small row of suggested words appears above the cursor as you type. Press the **up arrow** to move into it, choose a word with the **left** and **right arrows**, and press **Enter**.

## Why word prediction helps with a tremor

Word prediction means typing the first few letters and letting the computer offer the rest. AbilityNet, a UK charity that helps disabled people use technology, describes it as a way to type less: you type a few letters, and the software suggests complete words.

Fewer key presses means fewer chances for a doubled letter or a neighbouring key to slip in. A long or tricky word, such as "appointment" or "prescription", can become four or five key presses and a pick from the list.

## How to turn on text suggestions

1. Press the **Windows key and I** to open **Settings**.
2. Select **Time & language**, then **Typing**.
3. Turn on **Show text suggestions when typing on the physical keyboard**.
4. If you type in more than one language, also turn on **Multilingual text suggestions**.

To test it, open an email or a document and start typing a word. Suggestions appear above the cursor and get more accurate as you type more letters.

## How to pick a suggestion without the mouse

Clicking a small word on screen is hard with a shaky hand, so the keyboard is usually easier:

1. While the suggestions are showing, press the **up arrow** key. The first suggestion is highlighted.
2. Press the **left** or **right arrow** to move to the word you want.
3. Press **Enter** to insert it.

To get rid of the suggestions without choosing one, press **Esc**.

If you'd rather click, you can select a suggestion with the mouse or by touch. Our [guide to bigger, easier-to-follow pointers](/bigger-mouse-pointer) can help with aiming.

## Where it works, and where it doesn't

Microsoft gives a Teams chat, a Word document and a comment box in a web browser as examples. It works in many apps, but not in every one: some apps have their own prediction or don't show Windows suggestions at all. If you don't see suggestions somewhere, try typing in Notepad to check that the setting is on.

## Getting the most from it

- **Type the start of the word carefully, then look up.** Two or three correct letters are usually enough for a good suggestion.
- **Combine it with Filter Keys.** [Filter Keys](/filter-keys-windows-11) stops doubled letters, which also keeps suggestions accurate, because a typo in the first letters sends the guesses the wrong way.
- **Use your voice for long messages.** For longer writing, [voice typing](/voice-typing-windows-11) can save even more key presses.

## On a phone or tablet

Most smartphones offer predictive text by default, as AbilityNet notes, so you may already be using it there. The Windows setting brings the same idea to your PC.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why don't I see any suggestions?**
Check that **Show text suggestions when typing on the physical keyboard** is on, not only a touch keyboard setting. Then try in Notepad: some apps don't show Windows suggestions.

**Does it work in Word?**
Yes, Microsoft lists a Word document as one of the places it works.

**Is it the same as autocorrect?**
No. Text suggestions offer words for you to choose; nothing changes unless you pick one.

**Can I turn it off again?**
Yes. Switch off the same setting under **Settings** > **Time & language** > **Typing**.

## A note on your health

Word prediction makes typing easier; it doesn't treat a tremor. If your tremor is new, getting worse or affecting daily life, talk to your doctor.
