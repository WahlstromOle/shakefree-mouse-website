## What Mouse Keys does

With **Mouse keys** turned on, the number pad on the right of the keyboard controls the pointer. The keys around **5** move it, **5** clicks, and a few other keys handle right-clicks, double-clicks and dragging. You don't need to hold anything steady: each key press is a deliberate, separate movement.

You need a keyboard with a **number pad**. Many laptops don't have one; a separate USB number pad is inexpensive and works the same way.

## How to turn on Mouse Keys in Windows 11

1. Press the **Windows key and U** to open **Settings** > **Accessibility**.
2. Under **Interaction**, select **Mouse**.
3. Turn on **Mouse keys**.

<!-- SCREENSHOT: Settings > Accessibility > Mouse with the Mouse keys switch on and its options shown below -->

On the same page you'll find options worth setting straight away:

- **Only use mouse keys when Num lock is on.** With this on, the **Num Lock** key switches the number pad between typing numbers and moving the pointer. It's an easy way to flip between the two.
- **Mouse keys speed** and **Mouse keys acceleration.** Speed sets how fast the pointer goes when you hold a key down; acceleration sets how quickly it gets up to that speed. With a tremor, a slower speed and gentle acceleration usually feel more controlled, because a key held a fraction too long won't send the pointer flying.
- **Hold the Ctrl key to speed up and the Shift key to slow down.** This gives you a fast mode for crossing the screen and a slow mode for small targets, without changing settings.
- **Show the mouse keys icon on the taskbar,** so you can see at a glance whether it's on.

There's also a keyboard shortcut to switch Mouse keys on and off: **left Alt, left Shift and Num Lock** together. If pressing three keys at once is hard, you can leave it off and use Settings instead.

## The keys

All of these are on the number pad.

**Moving the pointer**

| Key | Moves the pointer |
|---|---|
| 8 | Up |
| 2 | Down |
| 4 | Left |
| 6 | Right |
| 7 | Up and left |
| 9 | Up and right |
| 1 | Down and left |
| 3 | Down and right |

Tap a key to move a small step. Hold it to keep moving.

**Clicking**

| To | Do this |
|---|---|
| Click | Press **5** |
| Double-click | Press **+** (plus) |
| Right-click | Press **−** (minus) to choose the right button, then **5** |
| Go back to left-clicking | Press **/** (forward slash) |

The button you choose stays active until you choose another, so after a right-click, remember **/** to go back to normal clicks.

**Dragging**

1. Point at the item and press **0** to press and hold the button.
2. Move the pointer with the number keys.
3. Press **.** (the decimal point) to let go.

This is very much like [ClickLock](/clicklock-windows-11): no need to keep anything held down while you move.

## Using Mouse Keys alongside your mouse

You don't have to choose one or the other. Mouse keys and the mouse work at the same time, and many people find a mix works best:

- Use the **mouse** for big movements across the screen.
- Switch to the **number keys** for the final small movements onto a tiny button, a link, or the gap between two letters.
- Press **5** to click, so the click doesn't nudge the mouse.

Hold **Shift** while pressing a direction key (if you turned that option on) for extra-small steps on fiddly targets.

## If Mouse Keys feels too slow

- Turn up **Mouse keys speed** a little, or hold **Ctrl** for long moves.
- Combine it with **voice access**: saying "show grid" lets you jump close to a spot by voice, then Mouse keys finishes the job. Our guide to [Windows 11 accessibility settings for hand tremors](/windows-11-accessibility-tremor) explains voice access.
- Use the keyboard to skip the pointer altogether where you can: **Tab** moves between buttons and **Enter** presses them.

## Frequently asked questions

**Mouse Keys is on, but the number pad just types numbers.**
Press **Num Lock**. If **Only use mouse keys when Num lock is on** is ticked, Num Lock has to be on for the keys to move the pointer.

**Can I use Mouse Keys on a laptop without a number pad?**
Mouse keys is built for the number pad. A small USB number pad is the simplest solution.

**Can Mouse Keys scroll?**
No. Use **Page Up**, **Page Down**, the **arrow keys** or the **Spacebar** to scroll most pages.

**My clicks have turned into right-clicks.**
You probably pressed **−** at some point. Press **/** to switch back to the left button.

## A note on your health

Mouse keys is a way to work around a shaky hand, not a treatment. If your tremor is new, changing, or affecting everyday life, talk to your doctor. An occupational therapist can also help you find the right mix of tools. For more ideas, see our [guide to using a mouse with a hand tremor](/mouse-hand-tremor).
