To make the mouse pointer bigger in Windows 11, press the **Windows key and U**, select **Mouse pointer and touch**, and drag the **Size** slider to the right. The pointer grows as you move the slider, so you can stop at the size that suits you.

The rest of this guide covers the choices on that page in more detail, plus a few settings elsewhere in Windows that make the pointer easier to follow.

## Choose the right size

The **Size** slider goes from 1 (the normal size) up to 15. A few things to keep in mind:

- **Try a size, then use it for a while.** Start a few steps up from 1 and go bigger only if you still lose the pointer. The right size is personal.
- **Very large pointers can cover what you're aiming at,** for example a small word in a document. If that happens, go down a step or two.
- **Only the tip counts.** However big the pointer is, a click lands where the very tip of the arrow points. A bigger pointer makes the tip easier to see, but the button you're aiming at stays the same size. To make the targets themselves bigger, raise the display scale (see our [main mouse guide](/mouse-hand-tremor)).

## Pick a style and colour that stand out

Under **Mouse pointer style** there are four choices:

- **White:** the standard pointer. Easy to lose on white web pages and documents.
- **Black:** easier on white pages, harder on dark ones.
- **Inverted:** the pointer changes colour depending on what's behind it, so it stays visible on light and dark backgrounds. Some people love it; others find it distracting.
- **Custom:** a coloured pointer. Pick one of the suggested bright colours, or select **Choose another color** for any colour you like.

A strong colour such as lime green or bright pink is easy to spot on almost any screen. Try it alongside a larger size.

## Show where the pointer is with Ctrl

If you lose the pointer, Windows can circle it for you when you press **Ctrl**:

1. Open **Settings** (press the **Windows key and I**).
2. Go to **Bluetooth & devices** > **Mouse**.
3. Under **Related settings**, select **Additional mouse settings**.
4. On the **Pointer Options** tab, tick **Show location of pointer when I press the CTRL key**, then select **OK**.

Now a tap on **Ctrl** draws shrinking circles around the pointer. It's one of the quickest fixes for "where did it go?".

## Let Windows move the pointer for you: Snap To

On the same **Pointer Options** tab there's a setting called **Snap To**. When you tick **Automatically move pointer to the default button in a dialog box**, the pointer jumps straight to the main button (often **OK** or **Save**) whenever a box pops up. That's one less small button to aim for. Not every app supports it, so you may see it work in some places and not others.

## Pointer trails: worth testing, not for everyone

Pointer trails leave a short tail of pointers behind as you move, which makes movement easier to see. You can turn them on under **Settings** > **Accessibility** > **Mouse pointer and touch**, or on the **Pointer Options** tab with **Display pointer trails**, where a slider sets the trail from **Short** to **Long**.

With a tremor, trails can also make the shake look bigger, because every jiggle leaves a trail. Try a short trail for a day and see whether it helps or just adds clutter.

## Don't forget the text cursor

When you type, the thing you need to follow is the text cursor, the thin blinking line. Under **Settings** > **Accessibility** > **Text cursor** you can turn on the **Text cursor indicator** (a coloured marker on the cursor) and make the cursor thicker. Our guide to [Windows 11 accessibility settings for hand tremors](/windows-11-accessibility-tremor) explains both.

## Magnifier for small details

For very small text or icons, **Magnifier** enlarges part or all of the screen. Press the **Windows key and the plus sign (+)** to turn it on, the same keys again to zoom in, **Windows key and minus (-)** to zoom out, and **Windows key and Esc** to close it.

Magnifier helps you see, not aim: when the screen is magnified, the shake is magnified too. Many people use it briefly to read something, then close it.

## Frequently asked questions

**What's the biggest mouse pointer size in Windows 11?**
The **Size** slider goes up to 15, which is very large. Most people find a smaller size easier for everyday work.

**My pointer changed back to the normal size. Why?**
Some apps and games use their own pointer. Your setting comes back when you leave them. If it doesn't, check **Mouse pointer and touch** again after a Windows update.

**Does a bigger pointer make clicking easier?**
It makes the pointer easier to see and follow, which helps. But the click still lands at the tip, so it doesn't make small targets bigger. For that, increase the display scale or zoom in the browser with **Ctrl and +**.

**Which pointer colour is easiest to see?**
A bright custom colour or the inverted style works on most backgrounds. Try a couple and keep the one you notice fastest.

## A note on your health

These settings are about seeing and using the screen more easily; they don't treat a tremor or a sight problem. If your tremor or your eyesight is changing, talk to your doctor or optician.
