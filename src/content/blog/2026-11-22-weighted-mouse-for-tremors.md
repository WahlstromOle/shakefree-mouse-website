A heavier mouse steadies the hand for some people with a tremor, but not for everyone, and the research on weights is mixed. No study we could find has tested the weight of a mouse. The sensible approach is to **test extra weight cheaply before you spend money**, and to treat it as one option among several.

## The idea behind adding weight

A heavier object is harder to set moving, so the same small shake moves it less. That's the thinking behind weighted cutlery, heavier cups and wrist weights, and it's why a heavy mouse is so often suggested.

Weights are a recognised option. Mayo Clinic says occupational therapists may suggest adaptive devices for essential tremor, including **heavier glasses and utensils** and **wrist weights**. An article on essential tremor in children, written by a neurologist and an occupational therapist for the International Essential Tremor Foundation, likewise lists weighting utensils, writing tools or a limb among the things occupational therapists try.

## What the research says

The evidence is thinner than the popularity of the advice suggests.

- **A trial in Parkinson's found no effect.** Researchers measured hand tremor in 16 people with Parkinson's while they held a spoon. They compared an ordinary built-up spoon, a weighted spoon, and the ordinary spoon with a weighted wrist cuff. The weights made no significant difference to the size or speed of the tremor, and the authors concluded that their findings did not support recommending weighted utensils or wrist cuffs for this kind of tremor in Parkinson's (Meshack and Norman, 2002).
- **For essential tremor, good trials are scarce.** A 2024 review of devices and mechanical approaches for essential tremor noted that, although many such products are on sale, most have limited or no published clinical trial data (Adabi and Ondo, 2024).

Two cautions about reading this. The Parkinson's study was small, looked at one kind of tremor, and tested spoons, not mice. And "no good trials" doesn't mean "doesn't work"; it means nobody knows how often it works, or for whom. Tremors differ, and so do people's responses.

## What this means for your mouse

Putting it plainly: a heavier mouse **might** help you, the only way to know is to try, and it's not worth paying much to find out.

A few things are specific to a mouse:

- **Weight doesn't remove a tremor.** At best it damps the shake, so don't expect a still pointer.
- **A heavier mouse takes more effort to move.** Notice whether your hand, wrist or shoulder tires sooner. If it does, the trade isn't worth it.
- **Weight doesn't fix clicking.** Pressing a button can still nudge the mouse or produce a double-click.

## How to test it cheaply

1. **Try a heavier mouse you already have.** An old mouse, or one that takes batteries, may weigh noticeably more than a light modern one. Use it for a day and compare.
2. **Try a mouse with removable weights.** Some gaming mice come with small weights you can add or take out, which lets you find the amount that suits you, or prove it makes no difference.
3. **Ask about wrist weights.** These are among the devices Mayo Clinic says an occupational therapist might suggest. A therapist can advise whether they're suitable for you and how heavy to go. Check with your doctor or therapist before you use them.
4. **Judge by results, not feel.** Pick two or three small tasks, such as clicking a small close button or selecting a line of text, and see whether you manage them more reliably.
5. **Give it a few days,** on good days and bad days.

## If weight doesn't help

Extra weight is only one way to steady a pointer, and several others don't depend on your arm strength:

- **Slow the pointer down** in Windows, so the same shake moves the pointer less. The steps are in our [guide to using a mouse with a hand tremor](/mouse-hand-tremor).
- **Support your forearm** on the desk or an armrest.
- **Try a trackball,** which stays still while you click. See [Trackball vs mouse for a hand tremor](/trackball-for-tremors).
- **Try a different shape.** See [Does a vertical mouse help with a tremor?](/vertical-mouse-tremor)
- **Filter the shake with software.** Smoothing software takes the tremor out of the pointer's movement without adding any weight to your hand. ## Frequently asked questions

**How heavy should a mouse be for a tremor?**
There's no researched answer. If weight helps you, the right amount is the most you can move comfortably all day without tiring. A mouse with removable weights lets you experiment.

**Are wrist weights better than a heavy mouse?**
Nobody has compared them for mouse use. Wrist weights are something occupational therapists sometimes suggest, so ask one before buying.

**Do weighted mice help with Parkinson's tremor?**
It isn't known. The one trial described above found that weights didn't reduce hand tremor in people with Parkinson's holding a spoon, but it didn't test mice.

**Can I add weight to my own mouse?**
You can, but a mouse built to take weights is simpler and easy to change back. Don't open a mouse that is under warranty.

## A note on your health

Nothing here is medical advice. Weights don't treat a tremor, and they aren't suitable for everyone. If your tremor is new, getting worse or affecting daily life, talk to your doctor, who can also refer you to an occupational therapist.
